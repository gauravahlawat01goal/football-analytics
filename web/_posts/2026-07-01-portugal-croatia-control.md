<p class="article-dek">Portugal have looked more convincing when Vitinha, Bruno Fernandes and Bernardo Silva dictate the rhythm around Cristiano Ronaldo. Croatia's route to an upset is to make the match slower, narrower and more uncomfortable than Portugal want.</p>

Match facts: Portugal vs Croatia, World Cup Round of 32, Thursday, July 2, 2026, Toronto Stadium (BMO Field), 7 p.m. ET.

Cristiano Ronaldo and Luka Modrić make this tie almost too easy to sell. One is 41, still Portugal's captain and the reference point in the penalty area; the other is 40, still Croatia's captain, still discussed by Portuguese players with professional reverence. In Toronto, one of them leaves the World Cup, and the wider coverage has run hard toward that: former Real Madrid team-mates, one last meeting, a ticket market inflated by the chance of seeing both on the same field again.

That is the poster. It is not quite the game.

The game is about whether Portugal can turn possession into control. Their group stage was not a straight line from talent to authority: a 1-1 draw with Congo DR, a 5-0 thrashing of Uzbekistan, then a 0-0 draw with Colombia in which they lost the rhythm of the match badly enough that the discussion around Roberto Martínez's team turned from status to structure. Portugal advanced as runners-up, and the language from camp since has been about correction - a cleaner midfield rhythm, and making the knockouts feel like a reset. João Félix said it plainly: Portugal let Colombia's game take over, and without control they made themselves uncomfortable.

The Wyscout profile supports Portugal as the stronger side, but not an untouchable one. Across the group stage they averaged 1.52 expected goals and 11.3 shots to Croatia's 0.93 xG and eight; they pressed more aggressively - a PPDA of 10.95 to Croatia's 17.79 (passes allowed per defensive action, where lower means a more aggressive press) - and conceded less chance quality, at 1.08 xG faced per match to 1.71. What the numbers do not erase is the question Colombia raised: what happens when Portugal lose the ball, and the first seconds after that loss are not clean?

That is where the Ronaldo debate belongs - not as a referendum on whether he should play. Martínez is clear that Ronaldo remains the final piece of his attacking model: the player who pins defenders, attacks the last movement and gives Portugal elite penalty-box presence. But El País framed Portugal's plan as increasingly tailored to Ronaldo as the reference striker, with Colombia exposing the trade-off: his box value still scares opponents, but the team behind him has to protect the press, the counter-press and the midfield connections with unusual precision. It is a team-shape issue made more visible because the striker is Ronaldo.

Croatia are well suited to test that shape. Their tournament has had more edge than volume: an opening 4-2 defeat to England exposed the defensive risk, then consecutive wins over Panama and Ghana showed a team that corrects course quickly. Their shot volume and xG lag Portugal's, but their progression profile points to a side that can still move the ball through midfield; the question is whether that becomes enough dangerous shots. Croatia do not need to dominate possession to make Portugal nervous - they need Modrić, Mateo Kovačić and the players around them to turn Portugal's loose possessions into longer spells, lower the tempo and make Portugal's forwards spend more time waiting than running.

Martínez knows the threat, describing Croatia as technically strong and adaptable - exactly the kind of opponent that punishes a favourite whose control comes and goes. So the first watch point is not Ronaldo's first shot but Portugal's first broken attack: do Vitinha, Bruno and Bernardo keep the game compressed, and does the counter-press slow Croatia before Modrić or Kovačić can face forward? Croatia's route is narrower but real: keep the score level, stretch Portugal's rest defence, and turn the match into a patient, attritional rhythm contest.

Portugal should be favoured - the attacking base is stronger, the defensive numbers cleaner, the talent around Ronaldo deeper. But Croatia's path is tactical, not romantic: slow the game, make Portugal's possession less secure, and ask whether the favourite can stay calm when control starts to feel like work. The poster says Ronaldo against Modrić. The match may be decided by everything that happens before either of them gets the moment the poster promised.
