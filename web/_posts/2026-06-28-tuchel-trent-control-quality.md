<p class="article-dek">Trent Alexander-Arnold being left out of England’s World Cup squad is easy to frame as a question about defending. But Thomas Tuchel may simply feel England do not need Trent’s attacking upside enough to risk changing a team that is already controlling games.</p>

Looking at data from Wyscout - England have created plenty without him. Across their three World Cup games, they have produced 8.27 xG, taken 56 shots and allowed only 2.43 xG against. That is an average of just 0.81 xGA per game.

The Ghana draw is the clearest example. England had 1.95 xG, 18 shots and 78.5% possession. Ghana had only 0.30 xG, two shots and zero shots on target. So this was not really a game England failed to control. It was a game England failed to finish.

That matters for Trent. His creative quality is obvious: at Liverpool he averaged 0.20 xA per 90 and 12.26 final-third passes per 90. But his biggest weapon works best when he has constant runners ahead of him. Liverpool had Salah, Mane and Diaz. Real Madrid have Vinicius, Mbappe and Rodrygo. England have excellent attackers, but not quite the same repeated threat running behind.

There is also a defensive trade-off. Trent’s numbers do not say he “can’t defend”, but they do show why a cautious tournament coach might hesitate.

So the fair conclusion is simple: this does not look like Tuchel saying Trent is not good enough. It looks like Tuchel choosing stability and pragmatism. Also reflected in his non selection of Foden and Palmer.

England are already creating chances. They are already keeping opponents quiet. Trent might add more invention, but he might also add more risk. For this England team, Tuchel may have decided the extra creativity was not worth losing control.
