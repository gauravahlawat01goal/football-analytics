<p class="article-dek">France can control this game and still give Sweden exactly what they want.</p>

The danger is not Swedish possession. It is the first pass after France lose the ball: Alexander Isak, Viktor Gyokeres and Anthony Elanga turning pressure into a sprint. That is the match's central tension. France want long spells around Sweden's box. Sweden want one clean exit, one duel, one run into space.

The team rankings point the same way. France have reached the final third far more often, about 63 passes there per 90 to Sweden's 38, and they have a clearer box-access edge: roughly 24 penalty-area touches per 90 to Sweden's 16. That is the evidence worth keeping. France should be able to pin Sweden back; the question is whether they can do it without opening the escape lane.

France can push Sweden deep and make them defend one decision after another. If Sweden clear without connecting, the pressure builds. Dembele's hat-trick in the 4-1 win over Norway fit the broader profile: this is a France attack with enough individual quality to punish one late shuffle, one lost duel or one bad body shape.

But Sweden are not just a team waiting to resist. They have scored seven goals from 4.81 xG, close enough to France's 10 from 5.81 to keep the warning light on. France have the stronger attacking platform. Sweden have enough punch to make loose French possessions matter.

France's best route is controlled aggression. Press the first outlet, but keep the cover behind it. The rankings point to France having the more aggressive pressing profile, and that matters here. If they counter-press cleanly, Isak and Gyokeres receive with their backs to goal. If the full-backs and midfield jump without cover, Sweden get the space they came for.

Sweden's route is active, not passive. They need Isak and Gyokeres to make France defend different problems: one forward stretching and receiving into feet, the other contesting direct balls and occupying centre-backs. Elanga's value is the outside-shoulder run that changes the field position in one action. Sweden do not need control. They need clean exits, second balls and enough broken-field moments to make France defend backwards.

Availability matters, but it does not change the central script. The Guardian reports Marcus Thuram will not be fit because of a minor muscular issue, N'Golo Kante is projected for the bench and William Saliba has been managed with a back issue. That still leaves France with the deeper set of match-winners, but it reinforces the tactical point: superiority only matters if the game stays on France's terms.

The watch point is France's first reaction after losing the ball. Rush Sweden's first pass, and France can keep the match near Sweden's box. Let that pass stick, and the tie becomes a sprint. France do not need to slow down. They need the safety net in place.
